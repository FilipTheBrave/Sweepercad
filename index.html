Opening it the first time

Your computer will warn you the first time, because SweeperCAD is made by one person and not registered with Apple or Microsoft. That is normal. You only do this once.

Mac

  1. Open the downloaded .dmg and drag SweeperCAD into Applications.
  2. Double-click SweeperCAD in Applications. macOS says it can’t check it — click Done (or OK).
  3. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and scroll down to Security. Next to “SweeperCAD was blocked”, click Open Anyway.
  4. Confirm with your password (or Touch ID), then click Open Anyway once more. Done.

On macOS 14 or older it is quicker: right-click (or Control-click) SweeperCAD in Applications, choose Open, then Open again.

Windows

  1. Right-click the downloaded .zip → Extract All… (don’t run it from inside the zip).
  2. Double-click SweeperCAD.exe. If a blue “Windows protected your PC” box appears, click More info, then Run anyway.

Linux

  1. Unpack the .tar.gz and run ./SweeperCAD inside it. The README lists any system libraries it needs.

Needs macOS 12.3 or later, Windows 10 or later, or Ubuntu 22.04 or similar. Which Mac do you have? Apple menu → About This Mac: “Apple M…” is Apple Silicon, “Intel” is Intel.

Make it with AI

SweeperCAD works with the AI you already use — Claude, ChatGPT or one on your own computer. No subscription, and by default the app sends nothing anywhere: you choose what to paste where.

  1. Describe it — “a vase, 120 mm tall, wavy rim, 3 mm wall” — and press Copy for AI.
  2. Paste it into your AI and copy its answer.
  3. Press Paste the answer & run. The part appears, as a real solid you can keep editing, fillet and export.

Want it different? Type the change — “make the rim wavier and add a foot” — and go round again. SweeperCAD hands the AI what its script actually made, measured — sizes, volume, loose pieces, anything the build complained about — and pictures from four sides, so it checks its own work instead of you having to explain what went wrong.

Or let it run by itself — only if you choose to. Switch on AI settings, use your own Claude or ChatGPT key, or an AI on your own computer (then nothing leaves it at all), and press Do it for me: it asks, builds, measures, looks and improves, a few rounds, each one in front of you. Off unless you turn it on, and an AI on mark shows whenever it is.

What it costs: the copy-and-paste way works with the free accounts of Claude or ChatGPT. Do it for me needs either an AI on your own computer (free) or an API key from Claude or ChatGPT, which they bill per use — a few cents per go. How to set it up, in the manual.

Paper and card come out exact, with glue tabs, numbered seams and fold lines; fabric and leather as panels with a stretch map, seam allowance and notches. Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. No account, no licence key, no trial. It runs on your own computer and sends nothing anywhere. If it earns its keep for you, there is a Ko-fi button at the bottom; that is all.

Is it open source?

Not at the moment. The downloads are on GitHub; the source is not. Project files are plain JSON, the library is CC0, and everything exports to standard formats, so nothing locks you in.

How is this different from FreeCAD, Blender, OpenSCAD, Plasticity…?

Those are sketch-and-feature, mesh, code-first or direct modellers. Here every shape is a profile swept along a spine with a scale graph — one primitive that covers extrusions, revolves, lofts and sweeps — shapes meet in binary combine nodes, and a fillet belongs to the node that made the seam. Every curve stays editable; there is no history to replay.

Was it written with AI?

Most of the code, yes — with every feature directed, tested by hand and on real parts, and corrected by a person. The design is human; the typing was largely not.

Can I bring in my own files — and work with them?

Yes, as real parts, not just as something to look at. STEP, IGES and BREP come in as exact CAD parts: move them, scale them, add to them, cut with them, fillet the seams. A closed STL or OBJ becomes a solid that booleans like anything else, or can be read as a subdivision cage and turned into a smooth CAD surface that goes out again as STEP. SVG drawings load straight into the profile and spine graphs.

What does it export?

STEP, IGES and BREP (exact, for other CAD), STL and 3MF (for printing, 3MF with colour), OBJ (triangles or quad patches), PLY and GLB with vertex colours, and any curve as SVG or DXF. Flat patterns from the unfold tool go out as SVG, DXF and PDF at true size.

macOS says it can’t open it / Windows warns.

The builds are not signed yet. Mac: right-click → Open (macOS 15: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway). Windows: More info → Run anyway. The install notes under the download buttons walk through it.

It crashed, a fillet failed, a boolean came out wrong.

It is version 0.9.9.2 and it has limits. Send the .swcad (or a STEP) through the contact form below with a line about what you expected — a broken file is the most useful feedback there is.

Can I use what I make commercially?

Yes. Whatever you make is yours. Library parts are CC0 unless their maker says otherwise.
